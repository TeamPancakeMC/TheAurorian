Autumn Harvest. The morning light had yet to break, and the bright moon still hung in the western sky. The villagers were already gathering their tools and heading to the wheat fields. We had to harvest as much wheat as possible before the sun scorched the earth, avoiding the hottest hours of the day.

As the noise outside grew louder, Mother roused me from my dreams. Having reached the age for fieldwork, I naturally had to join in during these most critical days of the year.

Still groggy from sleep, I was being tugged and pulled by Mother as she helped me change into my farming clothes. The outfit was uncomfortable, covering everything from the neck down, and the only sensation it brought was overwhelming heat. I wanted to resist, but remembering that everyone else endured the same discomfort, I gritted my teeth, picked up my sickle and bamboo basket, and bore it.

The bright moonlight spilled across the ground, casting the entire village and distant mountains in a silvery-white glow. Judging by the moon's position, sunrise was still over three hours away. As a novice, I didn’t understand why we had to go to the fields so early, but it wouldn’t take long for me to fully grasp the reason.

And so, in my dazed state, Mother held my hand, and the two of us followed the village chief, surrounded by a large crowd of villagers, heading to the vast wheat fields. Under the moonlight, the fields appeared as a expanse of pale white. Up close, the wheat ears were heavy, bending the stalks and hanging down in thick clusters. This year’s wheat had grown well—it was bound to be another bountiful harvest.

Each household was responsible for a different section of the field. We had to wait until everyone had finished harvesting before collectively tallying and turning in the yield. This time last year, I was still fast asleep in bed, but now I was stepping into the fields, clutching wheat stalks and harvesting the grain that would sustain us. Living such a peaceful life seemed like a truly blissful thing.

However, my illusions were gradually shattered as the harvesting progressed. The heavy wheat ears were exciting, but the unruly leaves and dust that found their way into my clothes were anything but pleasant. Even a few brushes against my face left me itching unbearably. Before long, several red marks had appeared on my face—almost all scratched by my own hands.

Seeing me in such a state, the nearby villagers comforted me: “Tayir, it’s your first time working in the fields this year. You’ll get used to it. We’ve all been through this.”

“Yeah, when I first followed my old man to the fields, I cried and tried to run away, but I ended up getting a few hard smacks on my bottom.”

All I could do was awkwardly scratch my head—or rather, the hat on my head—and casually change the subject: “Uncle Kamo, even someone as capable as you got spanked?”

“Him? Haha, I grew up with him. He got spanked plenty of times,” Uncle Med teased from the side.

“Look who’s talking. I remember you causing plenty of trouble and getting beaten quite often too,” Kamo quickly retorted.

For a moment, laughter filled the wheat fields.

“Tayir, if you’re feeling unwell, make sure to speak up. You’re a girl—just do as much as you can,” Uncle Kamo shifted the topic, pulling himself away from the past.

“Absolutely. Don’t be like that kid last year who stubbornly kept quiet and ended up fainting from the heat. Gave us all a scare.”

“But this year is better. The weather isn’t as hot as previous years. I remember back then, we were dragged out to work even when the moon was still high in the sky just to harvest the wheat,” another person, their head buried in work, chimed in.

One advantage of having many people around is the liveliness. Once someone starts a conversation, everyone quickly joins in, adding a bit of fun to the tedious harvesting time.

To be honest, I did find the clothes unbearably hot. They were neither breathable nor sweat-absorbent. Before long, the inner layer felt completely stuck to my skin, damp and clingy, with no way to shake off the sensation. In the end, I could only try to ignore it and focus on the work.

Bundle after bundle of wheat was harvested and hauled onto wooden carts to be transported to the drying field.

Time passed slowly, and a dazzling white light gradually rose from the eastern horizon——The sun's came out.
